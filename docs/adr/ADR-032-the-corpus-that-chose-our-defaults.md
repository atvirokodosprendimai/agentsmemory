# ADR-032: The corpus that chose our defaults could not disagree with them

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Owner:** Zy
**Spec:** None — no spec stage; grounded in two eval runs taken the same day on the same commit and configuration, differing only in the questions. Full tables: `evidence/two-corpora-2026-08-25.md`.
**Cross-references:** `internal/config/config.go` (`Fusion: "rrf"` and `RerankWeight: 0.5` in `Default()`, both annotated as measured), `internal/palace/eval.go` (the arms and the `real` style), `internal/palace/recallstats.go` (`search_events`, the source of the real corpus), `docs/adr/ADR-014-the-shipped-default-is-the-measured-one.md` (the principle this record does not overturn), `docs/adr/ADR-001-recall-answers-or-abstains.md` (the saturation preflight), `docs/adr/ADR-002-anchor-the-lexical-score.md` (`anchored:ceiling`, built and never shipped), `docs/adr/ADR-030-a-blend-that-cannot-tell-confidence-from-noise.md` (the change this corpus declines to confirm)
**Invalidates:** nothing yet, deliberately. It puts two shipped defaults — `FUSION=rrf` and `RERANK_WEIGHT=0.5` — under measurement rather than changing them.
**Served-path change:** none. This ADR ships a corpus and a rule about which corpus decides, not a ranking change.

## Context

Two eval runs, same palace, same day, same commit `3eb4b33`, same ranking configuration, same `--pool 30`. Only the questions differ. They disagree about almost everything that matters.

| arm | paraphrase (n=30) | real (n=26) |
|---|---|---|
| `vector` | **0.644**, tied best | **0.580, worst** — worse by 0.10–0.38 |
| `hybrid` (linear, bm25=0.40) | **0.485, worst** | **0.818** |
| `fusion bm25=0.60 anchored:ceiling` | 0.508 | **0.821, best** |
| `rrf` | 0.533 | 0.721 |
| `production (Search)` | 0.592 | 0.694 — worse by 0.02–0.25 |

**Vector-only is the best arm on one corpus and the worst on the other**, and both verdicts are paired bootstraps whose intervals exclude zero. The rerank weight sweep is monotonic in OPPOSITE directions: 0.25 is worst on paraphrase (0.517) and best on real (0.761).

**The mechanism is not subtle once the questions are read.** The paraphrase corpus is generated BY a model FROM the drawers it is meant to find, so every question carries its own gold's vocabulary and sits in its embedding neighbourhood. The dense arm cannot lose. The real corpus is replayed from `search_events` — questions agents actually typed — and those are dominated by identifiers: `mutatesOnlyTempPaths`, `gitTreeRefreshKind`, `AIAGENTMEMORY_BIN_DIR`. BM25 matches an identifier exactly; an embedding blurs it.

**Two shipped defaults were chosen on the corpus that cannot disagree.** `config.go`'s `Default()` sets `Fusion: "rrf"`, shipped as the default by ADR-014, and sets `RerankWeight: 0.5` with the comment "chosen by the eval's weight sweep". On the real corpus, linear fusion beats rrf by ~0.12 MRR and a lower rerank weight beats the shipped one.

**This does not overturn ADR-014 — it is ADR-014 applied one level up.** "The shipped default is the measured one" is correct. What this record adds is that a measurement inherits the validity of its corpus, and a corpus generated from the answers is a fixture that cannot exhibit the defect. That is the same failure this repository names everywhere else, arriving in the instrument that decides the defaults rather than in a test.

**One finding replicated, and it is the one nobody chose from a table.** The closet prior costs on both corpora — ΔMRR −0.048 [−0.11, −0.00] and −0.039 [−0.10, 0.00], Δrecall@1 −0.067 and −0.077 — and that comparison is PRESELECTED rather than picked from the table it appears in. Same sign, same magnitude, two independent question sets. It is already `closet-boost=0.00` in production.

**And one shipped change is not confirmed by it.** ADR-030's sigmoid normalisation is neutral against min-max on the real corpus (0.666 vs 0.668 at n=54); `norm=rank`, the arm registered to lose, scores higher at 0.748 on the n=26 table and is inconclusive. The degeneracy sigmoid fixes is provable in isolation and the corpus declines to say it moves recall. Recorded here rather than left for someone to discover.

> **Amendment, 2026-08-26.** This paragraph originally read "scores identically to min-max on the real corpus (0.708 both)". That comparison was void: `serviceForArm` reset nine ranking knobs and not `rerankNorm`, so on the n=26 run — where the served normaliser was already sigmoid — the arm named `rrf+rerank` WAS `rrf+rerank norm=sigmoid`, and the identical rows were one arm measured twice rather than a null result. The n=54 numbers above are a real min-max comparison and carry the same conclusion, so the ruling is unchanged; the evidence for it is one run instead of two. ## Amended 2026-08-26 — T1 ran, and the actionable half of this ADR is retracted

**T1's verdict: the corpus inversion is real; the effect size it implied for `FUSION` was not. T2's precondition fails and nothing flips.**

70 recorded searches, 54 scorable, same configuration. Full table: `evidence/real-corpus-large.md`.

| contrast | n=26 | n=54 | survived? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vector` worst | 0.580, worse by 0.10–0.38 | 0.587, worse by 0.01–0.20 | **YES** |
| `rrf` | 0.721, worse by 0.00–0.21 | 0.636, **inconclusive** | **NO** |
| `production (Search)` | 0.694, worse by 0.02–0.25 | 0.660, **inconclusive** | **NO** |
| best arm | `anchored:ceiling` 0.821 | `rerank blend w=0.25` 0.694 | different arm |

The Context below says "linear fusion beats rrf by ~0.12 MRR". **At n=54 that gap is 0.011 and inconclusive.** The spread across `vector`/`rrf`/`hybrid` went from 0.580→0.721→0.818 to 0.587→0.636→0.647 — same ordering, four times smaller. The n=26 magnitudes were substantially small-sample noise, and the claim built on them is withdrawn. The Context is left standing rather than edited, because the retraction is the more useful record: it is a worked example of a dramatic table at n=26 that did not survive doubling.

**What survives and is stronger.** The closet prior costs on a THIRD independent corpus and resolves for the first time: ΔMRR −0.027, CI [−0.06, −0.00], preselected. Three runs: −0.048, −0.039, −0.027. And `vector`-only really is the worst arm on real queries, resolved at both sample sizes — so the corpus inversion itself stands.

**What this changes about the ADR's thesis: nothing.** "A measurement inherits the validity of its corpus" is exactly what just happened to this ADR's own measurement, one level down. The paraphrase corpus could not reject the defaults it chose; the n=26 real corpus could, and overstated by 4×. Both are the same lesson, and the second one cost a retraction rather than a served default only because T2's precondition was written before the run.

**The instrument improved in the way that matters most.** This is the first corpus of the three that is NOT saturated: 94% in-pool against 100% for both earlier runs, which is the state ADR-001's preflight names as disqualifying. Three of 54 answers were never retrieved by any arm — a retrieval failure no ranking change can reach — and `production` lost five more below its page cut. A corpus that can fail is worth more than a table that agrees with you.

## Amended 2026-08-26 (second) — the pool-width trial, and the first actionable result

A paired re-run of the SAME 54 cases at `--pool 100` instead of 30, pre-specified with both outcomes named by the previous run's own output. Full table: `evidence/pool-100-paired-2026-08-26.md`.

**The three unreachable answers came back. `in pool` went 94% → 100%.** They sit between rank 50 and 100, so the embedding places them near their question and nothing was fetching far enough. Not a corpus problem and not an embedding problem — a fetch-width one, which is the good branch of that test.

**Every arm gained from the wider pool except the one we ship.**

| arm | pool 30 | pool 100 |
|---|---|---|
| `hybrid` | 0.647 | **0.697** |
| `fusion bm25=0.60 anchored:ceiling` | 0.650 | **0.705** |
| `rerank blend w=0.25` | 0.694 | **0.730** (best) |
| **`production (Search)`** | **0.660, 8 missed** | **0.660, 8 missed** |

`Search` performs its own retrieval — `candidateKFor(limit, …)` is `limit×3`, raised to `RERANK_POOL` — so `--pool` cannot reach it. It is **structurally excluded** from a ~0.05 MRR gain every other arm collected, and its 8 misses are golds retrieved and ranked, then cut by the PAGE. `limit=10` removes three of them.

That is the first actionable result this ADR has produced, and it is about retrieval width rather than about either default the ADR set out to test.

**And it forces a caveat onto an earlier finding.** The closet prior's measured cost was substantially a narrow-pool artifact: −0.048, −0.039, −0.027 across three runs all at pool 30, and **−0.002 with Δrecall@1 exactly +0.000** at pool 100. Three runs agreeing was weaker evidence than it looked, because all three shared a pool width. The honest statement is "it does nothing measurable at a healthy pool width", not "it costs recall". It stays off either way.

**`rrf` now reads "worse by 0.01–0.16", and this record does not claim that as the fusion verdict.** It is a second look at a hypothesis this ADR pre-registered against chasing; the pool change was made for a different, pre-specified reason. The defensible number is the gap — `hybrid` 0.697 and `ceiling` 0.705 against `rrf` 0.645 — not a "vs best" verdict whose baseline is selected from its own table.

## Decision

**Make the real corpus the one that decides, and re-measure the two defaults it disputes — without changing them yet.**

**T1 — a real corpus large enough to resolve the ONE contrast that matters.** 40 of 721 recorded searches were sampled and 26 survived judging. Sample 70, for ~45 scorable and about an hour of machine time — a budget set by the project owner and worth stating, because "sample more" without a number is how a measurement task becomes indefinite. n≈45 is sized against `rrf`, whose paired interval currently touches zero ("worse by 0.00–0.21") and which is the knob T2 would flip. `vector` and `production` already resolve at n=26, and `hybrid` against `anchored:ceiling` (0.818 vs 0.821) will not separate at any reachable n and does not need to.

**T2 — re-measure `FUSION` and `RERANK_WEIGHT` on it, and ship what wins.** If linear still beats rrf at that n, flip it and record the delta; if it does not, record that too and keep rrf with a measurement that finally could have said otherwise.

**Why nothing flips today.** The evidence is one run at n=26, its gold is judged by a 7B model, and its winning arm was picked from its own table. Flipping a default that reaches every deployment on that basis would repeat the exact error this record is about. Twice on 2026-08-25 a change was made on evidence that did not survive contact — five of ADR-029's seven claims, and a retrieval diversity cap abandoned when the default traces showed the widening loop already did it. The third time is the one to get right.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Flip `FUSION` to linear now:** rejected. A 0.12 MRR gap at n=26 on a judge-scored corpus is a strong hint, not a mandate, and it is a served-path default for every deployment. The cost of waiting is one unattended run.
- **Keep the generated corpus as primary and add the real one alongside:** rejected. They disagree on the sign of the largest effect, so "both" is not a position — one of them is deciding, and it should be the one whose questions somebody actually asked.
- **Discard the generated corpus:** rejected. It is reproducible, it needs no telemetry, and it is the only corpus available to a fresh install with no recorded searches. It is a regression fixture, not an authority on defaults, and the difference should be written down rather than left to whoever reads the table next.
- **Treat the 14-of-40 unanswered queries as a recall failure:** rejected as unsupported. The judge sees only the retrieved pool, so those conflate "the memory is not there" with "the judge missed it". It is a real number and it needs its own instrument before it means anything.
- **Withdraw ADR-030's sigmoid default because this corpus does not confirm it:** rejected. It fixes a degeneracy demonstrable in isolation (an opposed pair tying at exactly 0.5), it costs nothing measurable here, and reverting on an inconclusive result is the same error as adopting on one. It stays, and the non-confirmation is recorded above.

None. No package changes, no new service, no schema change. The work is eval runs and a documented rule about which corpus decides.

## Implementation

## Consequences

The instrument that picks defaults stops flattering the dense arm. That is worth more than either default, because every future tuning decision inherits it.

Two shipped defaults become measurable claims rather than settled ones. They may survive; the point is that until now they could not have failed.

**A cost worth stating: the real corpus is only as good as the telemetry behind it.** It is drawn from `search_events`, so it can only contain questions somebody asked — it cannot contain the recalls that never happened because an agent did not know to ask. No corpus built this way can measure that, and nothing else measures it either.

## Out of Scope

- **Changing `FUSION` or `RERANK_WEIGHT` in this ADR** (permanent: T2 owns any change, and only after T1)
- **The 14-of-40 unanswered real queries** (deferred: `docs/adr/BACKLOG.md` §"From ADR-032" — the judge sees only the retrieved pool, so the number conflates a missing memory with a missed judgement and needs its own instrument)
- **Retiring the generated corpus** (permanent: it stays as a reproducible regression fixture that needs no telemetry, which is what a fresh install has)
- **A stronger judge than `qwen2.5-coder:7b`** (deferred: `docs/adr/BACKLOG.md` §"From ADR-032" — it bounds every absolute number in the real table, though not the arm-vs-arm comparisons, which all face the same gold)
- **Revisiting ADR-030's sigmoid default** (permanent: reverting on an inconclusive result is the error this ADR is about, pointed the other way)
- **Anything about the recalls that never happened** (deferred: `docs/adr/BACKLOG.md` §"From ADR-032" — no metric exists and no eval can see it)

## Risks

- **The real corpus has its own bias.** It contains the questions agents asked of a palace they had already learned to use, so it over-represents queries that worked before. It is not neutral; it is differently biased, and closer to the served distribution.
- **A judge-scored gold bounds every absolute number.** Arm-vs-arm comparisons are safe because every arm faces the same gold; "85% recall@5" is not.
- **The sample size is set by a time budget, not a power calculation.** ~45 cases may still leave the fusion contrast spanning zero, in which case T2's Precondition applies and nothing flips. That is a legitimate outcome; treating it as a failed run and enlarging until the answer arrives is p-hacking with extra steps.
- A run that dies halfway produces a partial table that must not be read as a result; the `n=` fence refuses it.
- **Both corpora are saturated in-pool**, so neither can settle a retrieval question — only an ordering one. ADR-001's preflight already says so, and it applies here too.

## Rollback

Nothing to roll back in T1. T2's change is two literals and reverting restores today's behaviour exactly.

## Follow-ups

- Re-examine every default this repository annotates as "measured" against the corpus it was measured on. Two are already named; there may be more.
- Make `--style real` the corpus the eval documentation leads with.
- **Ask of any fixture that picks a winner: could it have picked the other one?** The paraphrase corpus could not, and it took an inverted table to notice.
